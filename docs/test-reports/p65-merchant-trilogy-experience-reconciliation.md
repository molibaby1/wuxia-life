# P65 Merchant Trilogy Experience Reconciliation

> **Date:** 2026-06-28
> **Stage:** P65 Wuxia Merchant Trilogy Player Experience Reconciliation
> **Type:** Reconciliation — three-layer evaluation and priority ranking

---

## 1. Overview

This document evaluates the three player-experience layers of the merchant trilogy package (P58/P59/P61/P63/P64):
1. **Success-cost differentiation** — Do the three routes feel like they pay different prices for success?
2. **Success-shape differentiation** — Do the three routes feel like they succeed in different ways?
3. **Recap-line / destiny-sentence strength** — Can players summarize each route with a clear distinct sentence?

---

## 2. Success-Cost Differentiation Evaluation (P65-003)

### 2.1 Assessment Question
Do the three merchant routes already feel like they pay different prices for success? Or do they all feel like "the same merchant burden with slightly different flavor text"?

### 2.2 Cost Signal Inventory

| Cost Dimension | Apprentice | Tavern Hand | Peasant |
|---------------|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Entry cost** | 放下手艺身段，从匠人变成买卖人 | 靠人情面子吃饭，把人脉当资源用 | 离开土地，从靠力气到靠算计 |
| **Bridge-phase cost** | 合伙信任的风险，手艺不纯粹了 | 人情债的重量，引荐的面子 | 脚力的辛苦，走南闯北的奔波 |
| **Pressure-stage cost** (P64) | 合伙人与人情债，供货账期、销路分成拴住 | 人情面子债，老主顾期待、介绍欠情让巨贾负重 | 车马仓储债，运力、仓库、下属工钱让泥腿子不敢停歇 |
| **Cost label** (P63) | 手艺与合伙的担子 | 人脉与铺子的担子 | 粮路与买卖的担子 |
| **Origin sacrifice** | Craft skill becomes secondary to business acumen | Social grace becomes instrumentalized as business capital | Physical labor becomes stepping stone, not identity |

### 2.3 What Works Well

1. **Entry cost is distinct and felt** — Each bridge crossing feels like leaving something behind:
   - Apprentice: leaving craft purity behind
   - Tavern: turning friendships into business relationships
   - Peasant: leaving the land and the labor identity

2. **Pressure-stage expressions are thematically appropriate** — P64 did a good job of matching pressure flavor to origin:
   - Apprentice pressure = partnership debts (consistent with partnership entry)
   - Tavern pressure = social debts (consistent with network entry)
   - Peasant pressure = logistics debts (consistent with labor/trade entry)

3. **Cost label is memorable** — "手艺与合伙的担子" vs "人脉与铺子的担子" vs "粮路与买卖的担子" is a clean triad

### 2.4 What Feels Thin

1. **Cost is expression-only, not mechanical** — All three routes:
   - Reach pressure at the same structural point
   - Have the same severity of pressure (narrative-only, no stat difference)
   - Resolve pressure in the same way (progress to payoff)
   - No route pays a *different kind* of price that changes the game feel

2. **Cost doesn't accumulate differently** — Apprentice doesn't lose more crafting-related identity, Tavern doesn't burn more relationships, Peasant doesn't suffer more physical wear. The cost is described but not embodied.

3. **Payoff doesn't reflect the cost** — The cost of success doesn't show up in the ending or the payoff shape. Success feels uniformly sweet regardless of the bitter path.

### 2.5 Thinnest Route / Phase

**Peasant route is thinnest on cost differentiation.** Why?
- The "physical labor cost" is least visible in the magnate phase
- "车马仓储债" reads as generic business logistics cost, not specifically peasant
- The peasant origin's core sacrifice (leaving the land, abandoning labor identity) isn't reinforced in pressure or payoff
- Apprentice has "手艺" as a recurring motif; Tavern has "人脉"; Peasant has "力气" but it fades in magnate phase

**Thinnest phase: payoff.** The cost is established at bridge and pressure, but the payoff doesn't reflect what was sacrificed. There's no "you succeeded but at what cost" feeling.

### 2.6 Success-Cost Conclusion

**Verdict: ⚠️ Moderate differentiation — flavor is there, but the weight isn't felt.**

The three routes *describe* different costs, but they don't *feel* like different prices. A player would say "the text about the burden is different" but not "I paid a different kind of price for this success."

The gap is between **described cost** (expression layer) and **felt cost** (structural/mechanical layer). Currently all cost lives in the expression layer.

## 3. Success-Shape Differentiation Evaluation (P65-004)

### 3.1 Assessment Question
Do the three merchant routes already feel like they succeed in different ways? Or do they all feel like "you become a merchant magnate, just with different backstories"?

### 3.2 Success-Shape Inventory

| Dimension | Apprentice | Tavern Hand | Peasant |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Entry shape** | Craft mastery → partnership → business expansion | Social network → referral → shop management | Physical labor → grain trade → logistics empire |
| **Core lever for growth** | Skill + trust partnership | Relationships + referrals | Endurance + step-by-step trade |
| **Entry-stage goal** (P63) | 手艺学透、合伙商路已通，正谋划更大的局面 | 人脉已通、铺子已上手，正借助这些关系扩张 | 粮路跑通、买卖上手，正学着像商人一样思考 |
| **Payoff-stage goal** (P64) | 商路掌控，供货销路尽在掌握，手艺人靠合伙信任 | 人脉通八方，老主顾遍布，酒肆人脉就是商路 | 车马仓储物流根基已成，泥腿子一步一步走出来的根基 |
| **Age 40 identity** (P63) | 手艺为基，合伙为径，商路是技能延伸的版图 | 人脉为基，引荐为径，商路是人情往来的延伸 | 力气为基，跑商为径，商路是勤劳致富的通道 |
| **Final gate** | `merchant_magnate` | `merchant_magnate` | `merchant_magnate` |

### 3.3 What Works Well

1. **Entry shape is strongly differentiated** — P63 did excellent work here:
   - Apprentice: "手艺为基，合伙为径" — business as skill extension
   - Tavern: "人脉为基，引荐为径" — business as relationship extension
   - Peasant: "力气为基，跑商为径" — business as labor elevation

2. **Payoff flavor matches origin** — P64 payoff expressions are well-matched:
   - Apprentice payoff = trade network mastery (extension of partnership pattern)
   - Tavern payoff = social capital value (extension of network pattern)
   - Peasant payoff = physical infrastructure (extension of labor pattern)

3. **The "business as extension of X" metaphor works** — Each route frames merchant success as building on the origin's core strength, not abandoning it.

### 3.4 What Feels Thin

1. **Same structure, different decoration** — Despite different flavor, all three routes follow the identical success shape:
   ```
   on_ramp → midlife_pressure → payoff → merchant_magnate
   ```
   The shape of success (how many stages, what happens in each, the rhythm of rise) is identical.

2. **Payoff doesn't change the final state** — All three reach the same `merchant_magnate` gate. There's no:
   - Apprentice merchant who's more of a "craft guild leader"
   - Tavern merchant who's more of a "social network hub"
   - Peasant merchant who's more of a "logistics baron"
   
   They're all just "merchant magnate with [origin] flavor."

3. **Success shape is additive, not transformative** — The origin adds flavor to the merchant identity, but it doesn't *transform* what success looks like. The player doesn't think "I succeeded in a distinctly different way" — they think "I succeeded as a merchant, and I came from X."

4. **No distinct failure modes** — Relatedly, there's no sense that each route could fail in different ways. Success and failure are symmetric.

### 3.5 Thinnest Layer

**The missing force sits at the payoff/ending layer.** Entry differentiation is strong (P63 delivered well), pressure differentiation is moderate (P64 delivered but only at expression level), but **payoff differentiation is the weakest because it doesn't change the shape of success — it just decorates the same shape**.

The player's sense of "what kind of merchant I became" is thinnest at the end, when it should be strongest.

### 3.6 Success-Shape Conclusion

**Verdict: ⚠️ Moderate differentiation at entry, weak at payoff.**

The *narrative shape* of success is well-differentiated (you got there through different means), but the *structural shape* of success is identical (you end up at the same place with the same status).

The gap is between **flavored success** (same shape, different words) and **shaped success** (different structure, different feel).

## 4. Recap-Line / Destiny-Sentence Strength Evaluation (P65-005)

### 4.1 Assessment Question
Does the current trilogy give players a clear sentence they can use to summarize what kind of merchant life they lived? Can they say "I was a X merchant" and have that mean something distinct?

### 4.2 Recap Signal Inventory

| Surface | Apprentice | Tavern Hand | Peasant |
|---------|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Life memory (bridge)** | 你与买卖人合伙经商，从学徒踏上了商路。 | 你靠着酒肆积累的人脉进了城里的铺子，从跑堂伙计踏上了商路。 | 你从田间走到粮路上，泥腿子出身也做起了买卖。 |
| **Ordinary origin summary** | 学徒出身的商人：从铺子学徒到商路合伙，跨越了手艺与买卖的界限。 | 酒肆出身的商人：从跑堂伙计到城里铺子，靠人脉铺出了商路。 | 农家出身的粮货商人：从田间地头到走南闯北，靠脚力踏出了粮路。 |
| **Age 40 identity** | 你是从学徒走来的巨贾：手艺为基，合伙为径，商路是技能延伸的版图 | 你是从酒肆走来的巨贾：人脉为基，引荐为径，商路是人情往来的延伸 | 你是从农家走来的巨贾：力气为基，跑商为径，商路是勤劳致富的通道 |
| **Final destiny sentence** | None — just `merchant_magnate` | None — just `merchant_magnate` | None — just `merchant_magnate` |
| **Ending recap** | Generic merchant ending | Generic merchant ending | Generic merchant ending |

### 4.3 What Works Well

1. **Origin summary is strong** — The "X出身的商人" sentences are clear and memorable:
   - "学徒出身的商人：从铺子学徒到商路合伙，跨越了手艺与买卖的界限。"
   - "酒肆出身的商人：从跑堂伙计到城里铺子，靠人脉铺出了商路。"
   - "农家出身的粮货商人：从田间地头到走南闯北，靠脚力踏出了粮路。"

2. **Age 40 identity is well-crafted** — "手艺为基，合伙为径" / "人脉为基，引荐为径" / "力气为基，跑商为径" is a strong triad that players might remember.

3. **Bridge memory is distinct** — Each bridge crossing moment has a memorable one-liner.

### 4.4 What Feels Thin

1. **No final destiny sentence** — The biggest gap. When the game ends and the player looks back at their life, there's no distinct closing sentence that says "this was a [type] merchant life." It just says "merchant magnate."

2. **Recap gets weaker as you go later** — The trajectory is:
   - Bridge: strong distinct memory ✅
   - Entry: strong distinct identity ✅
   - Pressure: moderate distinct flavor ⚠️
   - Payoff: moderate distinct flavor ⚠️
   - Ending: no distinct recap ❌
   
   The recap line *fades* when it should *intensify*.

3. **No "one-sentence life summary" per route** — A player should be able to finish the game and say:
   - "I was a craftsman who turned his skill into a business empire."
   - "I was a networker who turned his friends into a business empire."
   - "I was a laborer who turned his endurance into a business empire."
   
   Right now, they'd all say "I was a merchant magnate."

4. **Ending screen doesn't differentiate** — The final identity is just `merchant_magnate`. The origin flavor is in the summary/memory surfaces but not in the final punchline.

### 4.5 Thinnest Route

**All routes are equally thin on ending recap, but Peasant might have the weakest distinct identity overall.** Why?
- "农家出身的粮货商人" is a good line, but "粮货商人" is less archetypically distinct than "学徒出身的商人" (craftsman→merchant is a classic arc) or "酒肆出身的商人" (social climber is a classic arc)
- "靠脚力踏出了粮路" is good but "勤劳致富" is more generic than "技能延伸" or "人情往来"
- The peasant route's success story ("hard work pays off") is the most culturally generic of the three

### 4.6 Recap-Line Conclusion

**Verdict: ⚠️ Moderate recap strength mid-journey, weak at the end.**

The trilogy *establishes* distinct identity well (bridge + entry), but it doesn't *consummate* it at the end. The player builds up a sense of "what kind of merchant I am" through the journey, but the ending doesn't deliver the satisfying punchline.

The gap is between **origin-flavored recap** ("I came from X, then became a merchant") and **destiny-shaped recap** ("I lived a distinctly X kind of merchant life").

## 5. Three-Layer Priority Ranking (P65-006)

### 5.1 Ranking Framework

We rank the three experience layers by:
1. **Current strength** — how differentiated it already feels
2. **Player impact** — how much improving it would change the experience
3. **Implementation feasibility** — how bounded an optimization would be
4. **Repo grounding** — evidence from existing code and content

### 5.2 Layer-by-Layer Assessment

| Layer | Current Strength | Player Impact if Improved | Feasibility |
|-------|-----------------|--------------------------|-------------|
| **Success-cost differentiation** | Moderate (expression only, no felt weight) | High — would make success feel earned in different ways | Medium — needs expression + possibly light structural changes |
| **Success-shape differentiation** | Moderate-weak (flavored, not shaped) | Very high — would make routes feel fundamentally different | Low — needs structural changes to payoff/ending |
| **Recap-line / destiny-sentence** | Weak at ending, strong mid-journey | High — ending punchline is memorable and low-effort to notice | High — mostly expression-level, can build on existing signals |

### 5.3 Priority Ranking

**Priority 1: Success-cost differentiation**
- Why #1: It's the layer where the gap between "described" and "felt" is most impactful
- Why now: P64 already established the expression foundation; the next step is making the cost *feel* real
- Player impact: When success feels like it costs different things, the whole journey feels different
- Boundedness: Can be done with expression intensification + light structural touches (no full rewrite)
- Repo evidence: P64 pressure expressions exist but are skin-deep; cost label exists but isn't reinforced

**Priority 2: Recap-line / destiny-sentence strength**
- Why #2: High impact, high feasibility — the lowest-effort, highest-visibility improvement
- Why not #1: It's a "finishing touch" layer — it amplifies existing differentiation but doesn't create new differentiation
- Player impact: A good destiny sentence is what players remember and repeat
- Boundedness: Almost entirely expression-level, building on existing identity signals
- Repo evidence: Summary and age40Identity already strong; just missing the final punchline

**Priority 3: Success-shape differentiation**
- Why #3: Highest potential impact but lowest feasibility and highest scope risk
- Why last: True shape differentiation requires structural changes to payoff/ending, which risks scope creep into full merchant wave
- Player impact: Would make routes feel fundamentally different, but requires the most work
- Boundedness: Risk of expanding into full merchant content wave
- Repo evidence: P63/P64 already delivered flavored success; shaped success is a much bigger lift

### 5.4 P66 Recommendation: Success-Cost Differentiation

**P66 should focus on success-cost differentiation as the primary cut.**

Reasons:
1. **It's the most impactful "next layer"** — Entry differentiation is done (P63), pressure/payoff expression is done (P64). The natural next step is making the cost of success feel real, not just described.

2. **It's bounded** — Can be done within the existing magnate skeleton. Doesn't need new events, new chains, or new systems. Can work through:
   - Intensifying cost expressions
   - Adding cost-specific payoff reflections ("I succeeded but I lost X")
   - Reinforcing origin sacrifice at payoff
   
3. **It builds on existing work** — P64 already defined the cost themes (partnership/craft debts, social/reputation debts, physical/logistics debts). P66 can deepen what's already there.

4. **It's repo-grounded** — The evidence is clear:
   - P58/P59/P61 bridges establish distinct sacrifices
   - P63 entry differentiation preserves origin identity
   - P64 pressure/payoff differentiation adds flavor
   - What's missing: the weight of the sacrifice being felt at the end

### 5.5 Why Not The Other Two First

**Why not recap-line first?**
- Recap-line is high-value but it's a multiplier on existing differentiation
- If success-cost is weak, a good destiny sentence would feel hollow ("nice words, but I didn't feel it")
- Better to deepen the experience first, then cap it with a strong ending sentence

**Why not success-shape first?**
- True shape differentiation is a much larger undertaking
- It risks expanding into a full merchant content wave
- Better to do cost differentiation (bounded, high impact) first, then reassess whether shape differentiation is still needed
- If cost differentiation is done well, it might partially satisfy the "different success shapes" desire
